What Are NAD+ Injections

NAD+ injections are a form of longevity therapy that supplement the body’s production of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+). NAD+ therapy may prevent certain diseases related to aging and extend lifespan.

“NAD+ doesn’t just boost energy—it enhances mitochondrial function, repair, and longevity by ensuring that electrons are efficiently transported into cells to make energy,” says Candice Knight, M.D., a board-certified Family Medicine and Integrative Medicine physician.

Types of NAD+

While NAD+ is typically administered as an injection or as IV, there are some alternative delivery methods as well.

NAD+ injections: Given as a quick subcutaneous shot, NAD+ injections are absorbed directly into the bloodstream, allowing them to take effect quickly.11 Injections offer a more flexible, time-efficient alternative to infusions and can be administered from the comfort of home.

NAD IV therapy: Infusions deliver NAD+ directly into the bloodstream for rapid availability, typically over several hours in a clinical setting. One pilot study found that a continuous infusion of NAD+ resulted in a 398 percent increase in plasma NAD+ levels after six hours, and NAD+ levels may continue to rise 7–14 days post-infusion.12 13

NAD+ supplements: Oral precursors to NAD+ like nicotinamide or nicotinamide riboside (NR) are commonly used to boost NAD+ levels. However, these oral supplements may be poorly absorbed and unstable in the gut, limiting how much actually reaches your cells.14

Topical NAD+: Skincare products containing NAD+ have hit the market, but research is still in early stages. An in vitro study found that NAD+ may help protect against both UV-related and age-related skin damage; however, its effectiveness in humans has yet to be tested. Furthermore, the study was funded by a skincare brand, so additional research is needed.15

Are NAD+ Injections Effective?

Early research suggests that by restoring NAD+ to more youthful levels, NAD+ injections may help slow cellular aging, improve cardiovascular health, reduce insulin resistance, and boost metabolic function.16

One study of 12 older and 12 young men found improvements in fatigue in older men and strength on a leg-strength test after a single dose of nicotinamide riboside—a vitamin B3–derived supplement that can raise cellular NAD.17 Younger adults didn’t see benefits. Some research indicates that delivering NAD+ directly into the bloodstream helps avoid the absorption losses that can occur with oral precursors like nicotinamide riboside.18 However, more in-depth comparison studies about the best type of NAD+ are limited.

Do You Need a Prescription for NAD+?

NAD+ injections require a prescription from a licensed healthcare provider, such as a medical doctor, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant. Since there’s no standard blood test to measure NAD+ levels directly, your clinician may evaluate broader biomarkers and symptoms to determine whether NAD+ therapy is appropriate.

Common reasons for prescribing NAD+ injections include chronic fatigue, brain fog, poor sleep quality, slow recovery, or signs of metabolic dysfunction—all of which may reflect depleted cellular NAD+ or impaired mitochondrial function.

While some people turn to NAD+ as a targeted, proactive approach to symptom management, others pursue it simply as a proactive way to support cellular health and healthy aging.

Are NAD+ Injections Safe?

NAD+ injections are safe when provided as a prescription and administered at the correct dosage. “NAD+ injections are bioidentical to the NAD+ naturally produced in the body,” Knight says. “NAD+ does not accumulate in the body, so there’s no known toxic dose, and has been clinically used for decades for neurological health, energy metabolism, and addiction recovery.”

You can buy NAD+ injections online, but since the Food and Drug Administration doesn’t regulate supplements the way it regulates medications, it’s important to source supplements, including NAD+ injections, from vendors you trust.

NAD+ contraindications

Children and women who are pregnant or breastfeeding should avoid NAD+ because there isn’t enough data in humans to establish its safety.25

People taking the following medications should consult a doctor before adding NAD+ or NAD+ boosters:

Insulin or other glucose-lowering drugs26

Chemotherapy or other cancer treatments27

Antihypertensives28

Anticoagulants (blood thinners, e.g. warfarin)29

Tips for Injecting NAD+ at Home

With a little prep and the right technique, injecting NAD+ at home is easier and more comfortable than you might expect. Here are some best practices to get you started:

Let the vial warm up: Consider removing your NAD+ vial from the fridge and letting it come to room temperature before injecting. Taking the chill off can make injections more comfortable.

Set the stage: Choose a clean, well-lit space with a disinfected surface. Lay out everything you’ll need so it’s easily within reach.

Clean hands, clean skin: Wash your hands with soap and water. Then, prep the injection site with an alcohol swab to minimize infection risk.

Rotate injection sites: Switching up where you inject (e.g., alternating thighs or sides of the abdomen) can reduce skin irritation or discomfort over time.

Inject slowly: NAD+ can cause a flushing or tingling sensation if pushed too fast. A slow, steady injection is usually best tolerated.

Store it right: Keep your NAD+ vials refrigerated between 36–46 degrees F. Don’t freeze and keep away from light.

Dispose responsibly: Always place used syringes in a sharps container. If you don’t have one,ask your local pharmacy or waste service about take-back programs, or simply use a rigid, puncture-proof container with a secure lid (like an empty laundry detergent bottle) until you can safely dispose of it.

Track your doses: Consider using a journal or app to record when and where you injected, how you felt afterward, and any changes over time.

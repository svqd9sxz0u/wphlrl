Sleep deprivation is a known trigger for sleep myoclonus, particularly in conditions like juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (JME). When sleep patterns are disrupted or insufficient, the brain's ability to regulate muscle activity during sleep transitions is impaired, often leading to more frequent or intense myoclonic jerks. Prioritizing consistent, quality sleep is essential for managing symptoms and reducing episode frequency.

What are possible causes of left buttock pain that radiates down the leg?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Left buttock pain that radiates down the leg is often caused by three main conditions: sacroiliac joint dysfunction, piriformis syndrome, or hip joint problems. Each affects nearby nerves or muscles differently, producing pain that travels from the buttock down the leg.

Sacroiliac joint dysfunction: Inflammation where the spine meets the pelvis can irritate nearby nerves.

Piriformis syndrome: A tight piriformis muscle compresses the sciatic nerve, causing shooting pain.

Explanation

Pain in the left buttock that travels down the leg can happen for several reasons. One possible cause is a problem with the sacroiliac joint, which connects the base of the spine to the pelvis. When this joint is not working right, it can irritate nerves and cause pain that moves down the leg.

Another reason for this type of pain is piriformis syndrome. The piriformis is a small muscle in the buttock that can press on the sciatic nerve if it becomes tight or swollen. This pressure causes pain to shoot down the leg.

Hip problems can also lead to pain in the buttock and leg. Issues like arthritis or injuries in the hip joint can cause discomfort that spreads.

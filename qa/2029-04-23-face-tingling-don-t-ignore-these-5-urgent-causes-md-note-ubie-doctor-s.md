Internal Medicine

Facial tingling, also known as facial paresthesia, can be caused by several factors, including trigeminal nerve irritation, sensitive skin, anxiety, migraines, vitamin deficiencies, or exposure to pyrethroid-containing insecticides. Common symptoms include a pins-and-needles sensation, numbness, or crawling feelings across the cheeks, lips, or jaw. Persistent, severe, or worsening tingling—especially when paired with visible skin changes like a rash—warrants evaluation by a healthcare provider to identify the underlying cause and appropriate treatment.

Because facial tingling can stem from causes ranging from mild and temporary to conditions requiring prompt medical attention, understanding your specific symptoms is an important first step. Explanation

Tingling in the face, also known as facial paresthesia, can be caused by different factors. One common cause is a problem with the trigeminal nerve, which affects feeling in the face.

[Ubie Doctor's](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/)

Another possible cause is sensitive skin, leading to discomfort or tingling that may occur with or without visible skin changes.

Other health conditions (such as exposure to a large amount of pyrethroid-containing insecticide) can also cause face tingling. Extremely rarely, a certain type of small stroke in the brainstem, known as a lacunar pontine ischemic stroke, can cause no symptoms except for tingling in one side of the face.

# What could green stringy poop in my baby indicate? | Ubie Doctor's Note

What could green stringy poop in my baby indicate?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Green stringy poop in babies is often normal and not a sign of parasites. Common causes include a foremilk-hindmilk imbalance during breastfeeding, iron-fortified formula, mucus from a mild cold, teething, or the introduction of green vegetables. While parasitic infections can occasionally cause stringy stools, they are far less common in infants than parents may fear.

That said, persistent green stringy stools, especially when paired with fever, blood, poor weight gain, or fussiness, warrant closer attention. Because stool color and texture can point to many possible causes—from harmless dietary shifts to conditions needing medical care—it's worth getting clarity fast. Possible Causes

Diet: The baby might have eaten something that changes the color of the poop, like green vegetables or foods with green coloring.

Digestion: Sometimes, the poop moves quickly through the intestines, not allowing bile to break down completely, which can cause a green color.

When to Worry

If your baby is showing other signs of discomfort or illness, like a fever or not eating well, it might be a good idea to see a doctor.

If the green stringy poop continues for a long time, checking with a healthcare provider can help rule out any issues.

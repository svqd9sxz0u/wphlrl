Does having a seizure mean I have epilepsy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

[Note Ubie Doctor's](https://neurocurrent.china-akan.com/)

Internal Medicine

A single seizure does not always mean you have epilepsy. Doctors typically diagnose epilepsy only after a person experiences two or more unprovoked seizures occurring at least 24 hours apart. Seizures can also be triggered by temporary factors like high fever, low blood sugar, head injury, or medication reactions—none of which indicate epilepsy on their own.

Because seizures can have many underlying causes, identifying the right next step is important. Explanation

A seizure is a sudden surge of electrical activity in the brain and can happen to anyone under certain circumstances. However, having a single seizure does not mean you have epilepsy.

Epilepsy Diagnosis:

Multiple Seizures: Epilepsy is generally diagnosed when a person has had two or more unprovoked seizures. An unprovoked seizure is one that is not caused by a known trigger, e.g. a high fever or head injury.

Medical Evaluation: A healthcare professional will look at your medical history and may perform tests to understand why the seizure happened.

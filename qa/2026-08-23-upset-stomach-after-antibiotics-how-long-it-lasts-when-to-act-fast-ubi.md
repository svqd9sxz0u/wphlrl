How long can an upset stomach persist after taking antibiotics?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Antibiotic-related upset stomach typically lasts anywhere from a few days to several weeks. Mild symptoms often resolve within 3–7 days after finishing the medication, while more significant digestive disruption can persist for 2–4 weeks as your gut microbiome rebalances. The exact duration depends on the type of antibiotic used, dosage, length of treatment, and individual factors like age, overall health, and diet.

Because stomach upset can also signal something more serious—such as a C. difficile infection or an allergic reaction—it's important to understand what's driving your symptoms. Explanation

Antibiotics can sometimes cause an upset stomach because they change the balance of good and bad bacteria in your gut. This change is called dysbiosis. According to recent studies, the duration of an upset stomach after taking antibiotics can vary. Some people might feel better in just a few days, while others might have symptoms that last for a few weeks.

Factors Affecting Duration

Type of Antibiotic: Different antibiotics can affect your gut in different ways. For example, clindamycin has been shown to significantly impact the gut, possibly leading to longer-lasting symptoms.

Individual Differences: Everyone's body reacts differently. Some people may recover faster than others.

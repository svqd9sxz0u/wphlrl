Published on: 7/15/2026

When does breast tenderness typically begin during pregnancy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Breast tenderness during pregnancy typically begins in the first trimester, often as early as 1 to 2 weeks after conception. This early symptom is caused by rising hormone levels—particularly estrogen and progesterone—which increase blood flow and trigger changes in breast tissue to prepare the body for breastfeeding. For many people, sore or tender breasts are one of the first noticeable signs of pregnancy.

Because breast tenderness can also stem from other causes like your menstrual cycle, hormonal shifts, or underlying conditions, it's important to understand what's really behind your symptoms. Explanation

Breast tenderness is a common early sign of pregnancy. It often begins in the first trimester, as early as a few weeks after conception. During this time, the body undergoes many changes to prepare for breastfeeding.

[Tenderness Begin](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/)

Why Does It Happen?

Hormones: Hormones like estrogen and progesterone increase, causing the breast tissue to grow and retain more fluid, leading to tenderness.

Blood Flow: Blood flow to the breasts also increases, contributing to sensitivity.

What to Expect

Feeling: The breasts may feel fuller, heavier, and more sensitive to touch.

Duration: Tenderness often decreases as the pregnancy progresses, but it varies for each person.

Safety Considerations for Older Adults

Age itself isn't the problem. Health status is.

Before starting Stendra or Viagra, seniors should evaluate:

Cardiovascular health

Blood pressure stability

Kidney and liver function

Medication interactions

History of stroke or heart attack

ED can sometimes be an early warning sign of vascular disease. If erections have recently changed, that's worth discussing with a doctor.

Dosing Differences in Seniors

Older men often start at lower doses.

Viagra:

Typical starting dose: 50 mg

Many seniors start at 25 mg

Stendra:

Typical starting dose: 100 mg

Some older adults may start lower depending on health factors

Lower starting doses reduce the risk of dizziness and blood pressure drops.

What About Serious Risks?

Both medications can rarely cause:

Sudden vision loss

Sudden hearing loss

Priapism (erection lasting over 4 hours)

Severe drop in blood pressure

These are uncommon but serious. Seek immediate medical care if they occur.

Cost Considerations

For many seniors on fixed incomes:

Generic sildenafil (Viagra) is usually less expensive.

Stendra may cost more, depending on insurance coverage.

Cost alone doesn't determine effectiveness, but it can influence long-term use.

Lifestyle Still Matters

Medication helps, but it's not the whole picture.

ED often improves with:

Better blood pressure control

Diabetes management

Regular physical activity

Weight management

Limiting alcohol

Quitting smoking

Because ED and heart disease share the same risk factors, improving overall health can improve erections.

When to Speak to a Doctor

You should speak to a doctor if:

You have chest pain or heart disease

You take nitrates

ED started suddenly

You have pelvic pain or penile deformity

Erections last more than 4 hours

You have significant dizziness or fainting

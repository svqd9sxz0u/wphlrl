Salmonella is a type of bacteria that causes foodborne illness, most often found in raw chicken, undercooked eggs, and contaminated fresh produce. When these foods aren't cooked thoroughly or handled with clean hands and surfaces, salmonella can spread and lead to symptoms like diarrhea, stomach cramps, fever, and vomiting within 6 hours to 6 days of exposure.

If you suspect you've been exposed to salmonella or are experiencing symptoms, acting quickly matters—dehydration and complications can escalate, especially for children, older adults, and those with weakened immune systems. Explanation

Salmonella is a type of bacteria that can make people sick if they eat foods contaminated with it. Some of the most common foods that can have Salmonella include raw chicken, eggs, and certain vegetables.

Here are some key points about these food sources:

[Harboring Salmonella Bacteria Ubie](https://beautybiometrics.hbsnzj.com/blog/9013199087.html)

Raw Chicken: Chicken can have Salmonella on its surface. It is important to cook chicken thoroughly to kill the bacteria.

Eggs: Sometimes, eggs can have Salmonella inside the shell. Cooking eggs well can help prevent illness.

Vegetables: Some vegetables can be contaminated with Salmonella if they are grown in contaminated soil or water.

To stay safe, it is important to wash hands and surfaces often, cook food to the right temperature, and avoid cross-contamination between raw and cooked foods.

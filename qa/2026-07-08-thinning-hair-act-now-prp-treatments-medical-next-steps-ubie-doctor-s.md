Why Is Your Hair Thinning? Understanding Causes, PRP for Hair Loss, and Medically Approved Next Steps

Hair thinning affects millions of people worldwide. While it can be distressing, understanding the reasons behind it and exploring evidence-based treatments can help you take control. Below, we explain common causes, dive into PRP for hair loss, and outline medically approved next steps you can discuss with your healthcare provider.

Common Causes of Hair Thinning

Hair grows in cycles: a growth (anagen) phase, a transition (catagen) phase, and a resting (telogen) phase. When something disrupts this cycle, more hairs enter the shedding phase, leading to noticeable thinning.

Genetic (Androgenetic) Hair Loss

Often called male-pattern or female-pattern baldness

Related to genes and hormones (dihydrotestosterone, DHT)

Gradual thinning, usually starting at temples and crown in men, and widening part in women

Telogen Effluvium

Triggered by stressors: illness, surgery, high fever, rapid weight loss

Hair shifts prematurely into the shedding phase

Typically temporary; regrowth begins within months once the trigger resolves

Nutritional Deficiencies

Iron, vitamin D, biotin, protein and zinc are crucial for hair health

Poor diet or malabsorption (e.g., celiac disease) can lead to thinning

Hormonal Changes

Pregnancy, menopause, thyroid disorders can alter hair cycles

Elevated androgens (e.g., in polycystic ovary syndrome) may accelerate genetic hair loss

Medications and Treatments

Some blood pressure drugs, antidepressants, chemotherapy agents, and retinoids can cause shedding

Scalp Conditions

Psoriasis, seborrheic dermatitis, and fungal infections can weaken hair follicles

Physical or Emotional Stress

Chronic stress may shorten the growth phase, increasing shedding

What Is PRP for Hair Loss?

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy is an office-based procedure that uses the patient's own blood to stimulate hair growth.

How PRP Works

A small amount of your blood is taken, as in a routine lab test.

The blood is spun to separate platelets (rich in growth factors) from red and white cells.

The platelet-rich plasma is injected into areas of the scalp with thinning hair.

Growth factors in PRP promote increased blood supply to follicles and may prolong the anagen (growth) phase.

Benefits and Limitations

May thicken existing hair shafts

Can improve scalp health

Low risk of allergic reaction (uses your own blood)

Results vary; not a guaranteed cure

Multiple sessions (often 3–4, spaced 4–6 weeks apart) are typically needed

Maintenance treatments every 6–12 months may be necessary

Studies in journals like the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology report increases in hair density and thickness, but high-quality, long-term data are still emerging.

Medically Approved Next Steps

If you notice persistent or rapid hair thinning, here's how to proceed:

1. Get a Professional Evaluation

A board-certified dermatologist can determine if thinning is due to genetics, illness, nutritional gaps, or other factors.

A specialized magnifier (dermoscope) helps assess follicle health and rule out scalp disorders.

2. Blood Tests and Hormone Panels

Your doctor may order tests to check:

Complete blood count (CBC) and iron studies (ferritin, TIBC)

Thyroid function (TSH, free T4)

Vitamin D and vitamin B12 levels

Hormone levels (testosterone, DHEA-S, estrogen/progesterone in women)

3. Lifestyle and Nutritional Optimization

Include lean proteins, leafy greens, nuts, seeds, and whole grains.

Iron or vitamin D only when lab-confirmed deficiency is present.

Techniques like meditation, yoga, or cognitive behavioral therapy can help restore hair growth cycles.

4. FDA-Approved Topical and Oral Treatments

Over-the-counter topical solution or foam

Applied daily; may take 4–6 months to see results

Prescription oral medication for men

Blocks DHT, slowing hair loss and promoting regrowth

Off-label use in women to reduce androgen impact; requires a prescription

Realistic Expectations and Follow-Up

Most treatments take 3–6 months to show visible improvement.

Continuing topical/oral therapies and occasional PRP boosts may be needed to sustain gains.

Regular follow-ups with your provider to adjust treatment based on response.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Advice

Hair thinning is usually not an emergency. However, see a doctor promptly if you experience:

Sudden, patchy hair loss (alopecia areata)

Scalp pain or severe itching with hair loss

Signs of infection (redness, oozing, fever)

Rapid weight loss, extreme fatigue, or other systemic symptoms

Always speak to a doctor about anything that could be life threatening or serious.

Internal Medicine

Chest pain in the middle of your chest when breathing is often caused by respiratory conditions like pleurisy (inflammation of the lung lining) or pneumomediastinum (air trapped in the chest cavity). It can also stem from chest wall issues, such as muscle strain, costochondritis, or rib injury.

Explanation

A tightening or pain in the middle of your chest while breathing is often called respiratory chest pain. One reason for this pain could be pleurisy, an inflammation of the thin tissue (pleura) that covers your lungs and lines your chest. When you breathe, this tissue moves, and if it's irritated or inflamed, it can cause sharp pain.

Another possibility is pneumomediastinum. In this condition, air leaks into the central part of your chest (the mediastinum) and causes discomfort, especially when breathing deeply. Additionally, sometimes pain linked to breathing may come from the muscles or joints of your chest wall rather than your lungs, such as with costochondritis, where the cartilage connecting your ribs to your breastbone is inflamed.

Since these causes each affect breathing differently, it's important to have persistent or severe pain evaluated by a healthcare professional.

# Why Your Doctor Cautions Against Resveratrol with Blood Thinners | Ubie Doctor's Note

Why Your Doctor Cautions Against Resveratrol with Blood Thinners

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Resveratrol—a plant polyphenol in grapes, red wine, and supplements—can dangerously amplify the effects of blood thinners. It reduces platelet aggregation and inhibits liver enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2C9) that clear anticoagulant and antiplatelet medications, potentially raising your bleeding risk.

Several factors influence this interaction, including the type of blood thinner (warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel), your liver and kidney function, and the resveratrol dose. Bruising, nosebleeds, or unusual fatigue may signal a problem that shouldn't be ignored.

If you're taking a blood thinner and using resveratrol—or noticing symptoms that concern you—understanding your risk quickly matters. Explanation

Resveratrol is a naturally occurring compound found in grapes, red wine, berries and certain nuts. It's praised for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. However, when combined with blood thinners, resveratrol can affect how your body handles clotting. This interaction—often called "Resveratrol and blood thinners"—is the reason your doctor may advise caution.

What Is Resveratrol?

Resveratrol is a plant polyphenol. People take it as a dietary supplement to support heart health, circulation and overall well-being. In moderate amounts from foods or wine, it's generally safe. But high-dose supplements can have stronger effects on your blood.

How Blood Thinners Work

Blood thinners (anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs) reduce the blood's ability to form clots. Common examples include:

Warfarin (Coumadin®)

Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) such as apixaban, rivaroxaban and dabigatran

Antiplatelet agents like aspirin and clopidogrel

These medications are prescribed to prevent stroke, heart attack and other clot-related complications. They work by interfering with clotting factors or platelets.

Why Resveratrol and Blood Thinners Interact

Enhanced Antiplatelet EffectResveratrol has been shown to reduce platelet aggregation (the clumping process that starts a clot). When combined with aspirin or clopidogrel, it can multiply the blood-thinning effect, raising the risk of bleeding.

Enzyme Inhibition (CYP450 System)

Warfarin is metabolized by liver enzymes (particularly CYP2C9 and CYP3A4).

Resveratrol can inhibit these enzymes, slowing warfarin breakdown.

Slower clearance may lead to higher warfarin levels, pushing your international normalized ratio (INR) above the safe range.

Increased Bleeding RiskMultiple studies in journals such as the Journal of Thrombosis and Haemostasis and European Journal of Clinical Nutrition report that combining resveratrol with standard anticoagulant therapy can significantly raise bleeding risk.

Who Is Most at Risk?

Patients on warfarin (most sensitive to CYP changes)

Users of aspirin or clopidogrel (additive antiplatelet effects)

Individuals with liver disease or kidney impairment (reduced ability to clear drugs)

Signs and Symptoms to Watch For

If you're taking blood thinners and decide (with your doctor's approval) to try resveratrol, monitor yourself closely. Contact a healthcare provider if you notice:

Unexplained bruising or bruises that spread

Nosebleeds or gum bleeding

Blood in urine (pink or red urine)

Avoid Other Blood-Thinning SupplementsCombining multiple agents (e.g., garlic, ginkgo, high-dose fish oil) can further increase bleeding risk.

Balancing Benefits and Risks

Resveratrol's cardiovascular benefits are promising—but not risk-free when mixed with anticoagulant therapy. Your doctor's caution comes from:

The narrow therapeutic window of blood thinners

Variation in supplement quality and dosage

Potential for unpredictable interactions

By keeping open communication and following a tailored plan, you can enjoy the benefits of both therapies safely.

When to Seek Medical Attention

Seek immediate care if you experience:

Severe or uncontrolled bleeding

Dizziness or fainting spells

Chest pain or difficulty breathing

Any life-threatening symptom

What is a receding hairline?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A receding hairline occurs when hair at the front of the scalp gradually thins and moves backward, often forming an "M" or "V" shape. It is most common in men and typically becomes more noticeable with age, though it can also affect women. Causes include genetics, hormonal changes, aging, stress, and certain medical conditions.

If you've noticed changes in your hairline, it's worth understanding whether it's typical age-related pattern hair loss or a sign of something else. Explanation

A receding hairline is a common condition where the hair at the front of the head slowly moves backward. This is often seen in men as they grow older and is a natural part of hair maturation. The process can be influenced by genetics and hormones.

Key Points:

Asymmetry: Sometimes, the hairline doesn't recede evenly. One side might move back faster than the other.

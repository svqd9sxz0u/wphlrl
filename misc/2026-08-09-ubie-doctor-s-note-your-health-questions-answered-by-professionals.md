Understanding Empty Nose Syndrome After Sinus Surgery: Doctor Explanations

Empty Nose Syndrome (ENS) is a rare but serious complication that can occur after sinus or turbinate surgery, when excessive removal of turbinate and mucosal tissue disrupts the nose's ability to humidify air and regulate airflow. Despite an "open" nasal passage, patients often experience paradoxical nasal obstruction, chronic dryness, crusting, suffocation-like sensations, and impaired breathing comfort. Diagnosis typically involves an ENT specialist and includes airflow testing, nasal endoscopy, imaging studies, and validated patient questionnaires such as the ENS6Q. Treatment options span a wide spectrum, from conservative approaches like saline irrigation, humidification, and menthol therapy to advanced interventions such as submucosal tissue grafts, implants, neurostimulation, sensory retraining, and psychological support for the anxiety and depression that often accompany ENS. Because ENS symptoms overlap with many other nasal and sinus conditions, and early recognition can significantly influence outcomes, understanding what you're experiencing is the critical first step.

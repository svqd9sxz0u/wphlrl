What is a breast exam?

A breast exam is a medical checkup of the breasts. It is performed to evaluate any abnormalities, such as lumps or changes in the size or shape of the breasts. The exam includes visual inspection and manual palpation (feeling) of the breasts.

There are two types of breast exams: self-exam and clinical exam

A self-exam involves the woman performing a manual examination of her own breasts at home regularly. This should be done at least once a month, ideally after a shower when the skin is still moist and slippery. During the self-exam, women should look for any lumps, dimpling, or other changes in the shape and texture of their breasts. They should also note any pain or discomfort that they may experience.

A clinical exam is performed by a healthcare professional. It is a more thorough examination of the breasts than the self-exam and involves visual inspection and physical palpation (feeling) of both breasts and the surrounding area. This type of exam may also include imaging tests such as mammograms or ultrasounds.

Breast exams are an important part of preventive health care. They can help detect any abnormalities that may indicate the presence of cancer. Early detection is key to successful treatment, so it is important to have regular breast exams, both through self-examination and clinical examinations performed by healthcare professionals.

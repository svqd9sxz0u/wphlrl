# 8 Key Things You Should Probably Know About the Google Pixel 2

Not long after Apple announced its tenth anniversary smartphone device, the iPhone X, Google is gearing up to release its second generation Pixel phone, the Pixel 2.

Google's first Pixel phone came out in October 2016, and was generally well received. But with Apple and Samsung controlling a combined 40 percent of the world smartphone market, will the revamped Pixel 2 be enough to break Google through to the everyday smartphone user?

Based on everything Google’s told us about the Pixel 2 so far, it may have a shot. The phone is highly functional, and Google found ways to optimize its performance while also including some whimsy features. Here’s what you need to know about Google’s upcoming device.

The Pixel 2 is available for preorder now and ships on October 19. The Pixel 2 comes in your choice of blue, black, or white. If you want 64 GB of storage it'll set you back $649, and the 128 GB model is $749. Just like the iPhone 7 had the iPhone 7 Plus, Google’s phone will have a “big brother” in the Pixel 2 XL, a more expensive device with a larger screen and the same storage options.

There’s no headphone jack. Apple made waves when it nixed the headphone jack in the iPhone 7, and Google is following suit for the Pixel 2. Any Bluetooth headphones will work to pair with the device, but Google is also releasing its own line of branded earbuds, the Pixel Buds.

Those earbuds can do a pretty cool trick. They have Google Translate built in! Just tell the Google Assistant, “Help me speak Swedish” (or French or Russian or Swahili) to launch Google Translate on your phone. When someone types in a foreign language on your phone, their words will be pumped into your ears as speech in your native language. You can reply by holding down a button on the Pixel Buds and speaking in your native language; the phone will display the translated text and emit the corresponding audio. It’s amazingly close to real-time conversation across the language barrier.

The camera is powerful and knows some good tricks. The rear-facing 12-megapixel camera will not only capture detailed shots in a variety of lighting conditions, but it has editing software, too. A face retouching algorithm will automatically smooth away wrinkles and blemishes, and an “automatic portrait mode” serves to blur the background, cut around people’s hair, and generally liven up an image. Google’s Motion Photos are similar to Apple’s Live Photos. Capturing three seconds of video and one photograph at the same time, Google’s Motion Photos help give an image a little more context. The Pixel 2’s software will even find an optimal loop cycle, identifying the best startpoint and endpoint to create a high-quality looped video.

The camera even has augmented reality stickers. From kid-friendly food-inspired characters to the horrible monster that ran amok in Stranger Things, the Pixel 2 camera app lets you drop 3D animations right into your photos and videos.

There are some major cloud storage perks. A camera that does so much is surely going to see some use, so Pixel 2 owners get unlimited cloud storage of all their photos and videos. (Apple’s cloud storage is only free up to 5 GB.) If you’d rather not worry about keeping photos on your phone, but want to know that they’re still retained somewhere, then this is a valuable perk for you.

What Is Shilajit?

Origin: A sticky, tar-like substance exuded from rocks in the Himalayas and other mountain ranges.

Composition: Over 85 minerals in ionic form, fulvic acid (a powerful antioxidant), humic acids, amino acids, and trace elements.

Traditional use: Ayurvedic texts describe Shilajit as a "rasayana," or rejuvenator, supporting vitality and slowing aging.

Rich Trace Minerals

Copper: Vital for the enzyme tyrosinase, which catalyzes melanin synthesis.

Iron: Crucial for oxygen transport; low levels can impair hair follicle health.

Zinc: Supports protein synthesis and cell division in hair follicles.

Magnesium and Manganese: Act as cofactors in enzymatic reactions related to pigment formation.

Enhanced Cellular Energy

Fulvic acid helps shuttle nutrients into cells and supports mitochondrial function.

Healthy mitochondria ensure melanocytes have adequate energy to produce melanin.

Recommended Dosage

Typical adult dose: 300–500 mg of purified Shilajit extract per day.

Start with a lower dose (100–200 mg) to assess tolerance.

Timing

Take Shilajit with breakfast to support all-day energy and nutrient absorption.

Avoid late evening dosing if you're sensitive to its energizing effects.

Combine with a Balanced Diet

Ensure adequate protein, healthy fats, fruits, and vegetables.

Include foods high in copper (shellfish, seeds), iron (leafy greens, lean meats), and zinc (nuts, legumes).

Monitor for Side Effects

Mild digestive upset can occur; reduce dose if needed.

Discontinue if allergic reaction arises (rash, itching).

Complementary Mineral and Lifestyle Strategies

• Iron and Zinc Testing

Safety and When to Seek Help

Shilajit is generally well tolerated, but talk to your healthcare provider if you:

Have iron overload disorders (e.g., hemochromatosis)

Take blood thinners or immunosuppressants

Are pregnant or breastfeeding

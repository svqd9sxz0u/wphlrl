What is Tesofensine?

Tesofensine is an experimental drug originally developed to treat neurodegenerative conditions like Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s disease. During clinical trials, researchers discovered a surprising side effect: significant weight loss. This led to its repositioning as an anti-obesity medication.

Tesofensine is a triple monoamine reuptake inhibitor (TMRI), which boosts the levels of three key neurotransmitters in the brain: dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin. These chemicals are responsible for regulating appetite, mood, energy levels, and motivation. By increasing all three, Tesofensine helps decrease hunger, boost energy expenditure, and manage cravings, making it easier for people to lose weight and maintain it, especially when diet and exercise alone aren’t sufficient.

It’s currently being researched in clinical settings as a promising treatment for obesity, with results showing dose-dependent weight loss and minimal side effects compared to traditional stimulants or appetite suppressants.

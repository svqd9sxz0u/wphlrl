Wool Is The Best Fabric to Wear Between Seasons

Sure, wool can keep you warm like any other fabric, but it also helps regulate your body’s temperature in between seasons.

“Wool thermoregulates by constantly absorbing and releasing moisture vapor so the climate between your skin and your garment stays cool and dry,” says Michelle A. Lee of The Woolmark Company.

Look for lightweight (or “tropical weight”) and loosely woven versions of wool to wear between seasons. The deconstructed hopsack jacket pictured above is a great example; it also has minimal lining for maximum stretch and breathability.

Bonus: Because wool has high shape retention, it’s naturally wrinkle resistant.

Wool Can Be Worn Casually

You probably already own a wool sport coat, but that’s not the material’s only strong suit. T-shirts, base layers, and even underwear work great in wool.

Many casual wool pieces don’t require dry cleaning at all; they’re machine washable.

They don’t have to be scratchy, either. “Scratchy wool is simply poor-quality wool,” says Lee.

She suggests sticking to Merino, a kind of fine wool that feels great against your skin. (If a garment is Merino wool, it’ll say so on the label.)

The turtleneck pictured above is a prime example: Lightweight but cozy, it is an ideal layering piece cut in a classic slim-fit silhouette that will never go out of style. Get even more mileage out of it by choosing a neutral tone, like navy.

You Can Work Out In Wool

Wool is making a big impact in the world of activewear. (Check out some more of our favorite new workout gear here.)

Besides keeping your temperature stable and sweeping away sweat, the fiber is also naturally antimicrobial because it contains protective fats called lanolins. That makes midlayers like the one pictured above perfect for cold-weather outdoor runs, workouts, or hikes.

“Wool can also absorb a third of its weight in moisture vapor, reducing the amount of sweat—and therefore odor—left on the body,” says Lee. Read: It prevents stench and therefore requires less laundering.

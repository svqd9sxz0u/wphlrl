Understanding Yeast Infections

A yeast infection is fairly mild and may even go unnoticed for some, but it can lead to pain, dryness, inability to enjoy sex, and other problems, beyond the risk of further infection.

If left untreated, the yeast infection can lead to further infection of the vagina, particularly if open lesions are created due to scratching.

Yeast Infection Causes

Yeast infections are caused by an imbalance of fungus in the vagina. This fungus occurs naturally, but it can be thrown off balance by several different causes, such as taking antibiotics for some time, pregnancy, a weakened immune system, hygiene, and untreated diabetes.

Yeast Infection Symptoms

Yeast infections affect the vagina in specific ways. Women may experience pain, swelling, itchiness, rash, and vaginitis or inflammation of the outer area of the vagina. Women may also experience a change in their vaginal discharge.

Types of At-home Yeast Infection Tests

When testing at home, women have a few options for the type of test they wish to use.

The most common test is a pH balance test. This test is done by taking a sample of vaginal discharge and measuring the pH level of the vagina. This test is fairly accurate for women of childbearing age. However, it is not as accurate for women who have gone through menopause or those who have yet to start their menstruation cycle due to a difference in pH level at these ages.

Another common type of test is a self-sampling kit test. This one takes a sample of the yeast in a woman’s vagina and determines if it is a typical yeast infection or caused by another problem. Additional tests may be necessary to determine the exact cause of the infection and to administer the proper treatment, but this test can help to narrow down the potential issue before seeing a doctor.

How to Use an At-home Yeast Infection Test

A standard at-home test consists of a swab and a test strip. For best results, swab the outer area of the labia, applying enough pressure to press into the skin lightly. Next, insert the testing strip into the vagina and place it along the vaginal wall. Press tightly against the vaginal wall for around 5 seconds to ensure enough test material is collected.

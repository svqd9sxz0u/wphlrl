Ever wondered why your triceps simply aren't growing, no matter how much you're training them? You could be working the wrong part. The triceps are made up of three parts—the long head, the medial head, and the lateral head—so the exact target of the exercises you're doing might have some bearing on the effects on the muscle. According to Athlean-X expert Jeff Cavaliere, C.S.C.S. you can get maximum triceps gains by focusing on the largest area of the muscle: the long head.

As the long head is connected to the shoulder, Cavaliere explains, you'll want to be doing exercises that engage the shoulders as well. This can be achieved through overhead moves like the triceps pushaway. Technique here is important, and Cavaliere recommends doing the move in an upright position, as opposed to leaning forward, enabling you to get more stretch on the long head of the triceps in each rep.

"If you were just to stand a little bit more upright, and really try to get those arms up high on every repetition, you're going to get a better shot to make sure you can get more gains from the exercise every single time you do it."

Another useful exercise which places tension on the long head of the tricep is the dumbbell overhead extension. However, Cavaliere suggests switching out a bell for a plate to get even more benefits from it: "The plate gives me a hell of a lot more stretch on the triceps," he says, due to the shape of the plate which is less obstructive than that of the dumbbell. Additionally, holding a plate puts your elbows out in front of you—a safer position than when you're holding a dumbbell and they're sticking out to the sides.

In addition to stretching the long head of the triceps through overhead motion, Cavaliere says that getting a stronger contraction through exercises which move the arms back behind the body can also contribute to greater muscle recruitment. A variation of the tricep pushdown, with an offset grip, will allow that kind of backward movement.

"When you start to do things a little bit differently, the gains will be on the other side," says Cavaliere. "It doesn't mean you're going to stop bench pressing, it doesn't mean you're going to stop the close grip bench press, those are great ways to overload the medial and lateral head, but the real problem area is always going to be the largest area of the triceps that just isn't pulling its own weight. That's the long head, and now you've got an attack plan for that."

# Doctors Explain What You Need to Know About the Updated Covid Vaccine

THE UPDATED COVID vaccines are available now, and medical experts say everyone should get the shot.

The vaccines have been reformulated to match, as much as possible, the Covid variants that are circulating this fall and winter, and they’re a critical tool to protect you from getting severely sick with the virus, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

The shots aren’t referred to as boosters anymore, however. “The preferred term is the updated vaccine,” says Thomas Russo, M.D., an infectious disease expert and professor at the University at Buffalo.

The Food and Drug Administration is using the term “updated vaccine” because it expects to provide updated formulas every year as the virus evolves, in the same way the flu shot is updated annually, according to Yale Medicine. A booster refers to revving up someone's existing immunity from a past vaccine.

The current shot targets the omicron XBB.1.5 variant, which is the dominant strain, Dr. Russo says. “You want to get the vaccine that will protect you against the diseases circulating in the community.”

Getting the updated Covid shot can reduce the likelihood that you’ll become infected with the virus, he says. And if you do get Covid, being vaccinated will decrease your risk of getting a serious illness, being hospitalized, and potentially having long Covid.

So, the CDC recommends everyone six months old and older get the updated vaccine.

Everyone is also encouraged to get their flu shot, and you can get both vaccines at the same time.

However, only 47 percent of adults said they planned to get the new Covid vaccine, while 58 percent planned to get a flu shot, according to a Kaiser Family Foundation poll conducted in late September.

Here’s what you should know about the new Covid shots, their potential side effects, and when to get vaccinated.

What are the newest Covid vaccines?

The updated vaccines for the fall-winter 2023-24 season are based on the XBB.1.5 variant, and that’s the only Covid vaccine currently available, Dr. Russo says.

The current shot is a monovalent vaccine, meaning it targets a single variant, while previous vaccines were bivalent (targeting multiple variants), according to the FDA.

But, the FDA says the updated vaccines are expected to provide “good protection” against the Covid variants that are currently circulating, including omicron subtypes. The EG.5 (Eris), FL.1.5.1 (Fornax), and XBB.1.16 strains are also out there, but they’re closely related to the XBB.1.5.

What are the potential side effects of the new Covid vaccine?

No new side effects are linked to the updated Covid vaccine, says Vincent Covelli, M.D., a board-certified infectious disease physician at telemedicine platform PlushCare.

Experiencing post-vaccine symptoms doesn’t mean you have Covid, but that your body is responding to the vaccine, Dr. Russo says. “You might be getting a better immune response.”

A study posted in September that hasn’t yet been peer-reviewed suggests that people who experienced these symptoms after their second dose of a Covid vaccine had more antibodies against the virus one and six months later than people who didn’t have symptoms.

Who’s eligible to get the new vaccine?

The CDC recommends that everyone over six months old get the updated Covid vaccine.

Everyone who’s eligible should get the new, updated shot, Dr. Covelli says. People who are most at risk for complications from Covid, such as people over 65 or individuals who are immunocompromised particularly need to get vaccinated.

The vaccine is extremely safe, as well, Dr. Russo emphasizes. “The benefit/risk ratio is strongly in favor of receiving the vaccine.”

When is the best time to get the Covid vaccine?

“The best time is now,” Dr. Covelli says. “The sooner you obtain this vaccine, the more time your body has to develop an appropriate immune response.”

He adds that it can take a week or two to develop an immune response to the vaccine, so getting it as soon as you can will ensure you’re prepared for winter and holiday activities. And, people spend more time indoors during this season, which could increase the chances of Covid transmission.

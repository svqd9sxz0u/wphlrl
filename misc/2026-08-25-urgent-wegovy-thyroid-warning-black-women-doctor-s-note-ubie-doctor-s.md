Doctor's Note: Should Black Women Worry About the Wegovy Thyroid Warning? A Doctor Explains

Wegovy (semaglutide) has become a popular prescription for weight management. As with any medication, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) includes safety warnings in its prescribing information. One such warning involves the risk of thyroid C-cell tumors—based on rodent studies, not human cases. If you're a Black woman considering or already taking Wegovy, you may wonder about the Wegovy thyroid cancer risk in Black women. Here's an evidence-based, straightforward look at what we know today.

Understanding the Thyroid Warning• The FDA warning stems from studies in rats and mice, which showed an increase in C-cell tumors at high doses of semaglutide.• C-cells in the thyroid produce the hormone calcitonin. In rodents, calcitonin-secreting tumors arose with semaglutide exposure.• To date, no cases of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) have been linked to semaglutide in humans.• Animal findings don't always predict human outcomes. Other diabetes drugs with similar warnings have not shown increased thyroid cancer in people.

What the Research Says for Humans• Large clinical trials of semaglutide (including Wegovy) have not demonstrated any increased incidence of thyroid cancer in study participants.• Post-marketing surveillance (tracking adverse events after FDA approval) continues, but so far, no signal of increased thyroid tumors in humans has emerged.• Endocrinology societies do not recommend routine thyroid ultrasound or calcitonin monitoring for patients on semaglutide without clinical symptoms.

Thyroid Cancer in Black Women: What We KnowIncidence and mortality patterns for thyroid cancer vary by race and sex.• Black women have a lower overall incidence of thyroid cancer than white women.• However, mortality rates from thyroid cancer can be slightly higher among Black women, often due to later diagnosis or differences in healthcare access.• The most common type of thyroid cancer—papillary carcinoma—is usually slow-growing and highly treatable.• Medullary thyroid carcinoma (the type flagged in the Wegovy warning) accounts for just 2–3% of all thyroid cancers.

Common Thyroid Cancer Risk FactorsRegardless of race or medication use, the strongest risk factors for thyroid cancer include:

Family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2)

Exposure to head and neck radiation, especially during childhood

Certain genetic syndromes (eg, MEN2A, MEN2B)

Presence of thyroid nodules on physical exam or ultrasound

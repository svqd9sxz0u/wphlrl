# Conor McGregor’s “Apology” Is Seriously Pathetic

Conor McGregor posted a pensive photo of himself on Instagram, along with a caption about learning lessons

The fighter was charged with three counts of misdemeanor assault and one could of felony criminal mischief after violently crashing the UFC 223 media day

The attack left two fighters injured

Don't worry, everyone: Conor McGregor is doing just fine after violently attacking a UFC bus.

McGregor recently crashed the UFC 223 media day, allegedly throwing a hand dolly through the window of a bus and injuring two fellow fighters in the process. UFC lost three scheduled fights, and McGregor was charged with three counts of misdemeanor assault and one count of felony criminal mischief.

Now, McGregor has kind of, sort of, not really apologized for his actions in the most Conor McGregor way humanly possible.

Behold, the sorry-not-sorry gram:

"It is only a lesson if you learn from it. I learn every day," McGregor wrote alongside a photo of himself staring mournfully off into the distance, while literally twiddling his thumbs.

Well, that definitely settles that.

The statement via Myspace photo from circa 2003 constitutes McGregor's first public words since leaving Michael Chiesa with "severe" facial lacerations and flyweight Ray Borg suffering "multiple cornea abrasions." Given McGregor's outspoken nature, we will probably get many, many more words on the incident in the future, but for now we can all rest easy knowing Conor McGregor is learning a valuable lesson from the day he left strawweight champion Rose Namajuna so shaken, she walked a mile back to her hotel by herself.

While McGregor seems content with leaving this one to a sulky Instagram post, it’s interesting to note he has plainly apologized for his actions in the past. Some situations have been more serious than others, like his use of a homophobic slur last year ("I didn’t mean no disrespect"). There was also the time he jumped into a Bellator octagon post-fight and shoved a referee ("I sincerely apologize"), and the time he got punched by Floyd Mayweather too many times ("I'm sorry").

And then there's McGregor's most famous apology, from a post-fight interview during UFC 205.

"I've spent a lot of time... slating everybody in the company," he told Joe Rogan.

He continued:

Backstage I'm starting fights over everybody. I've ridiculed everyone on the roster. I just want to say from the body of me heart, I'd like to take this chance to apologize to absolutely nobody. The double champ does what the fuck he wants!

The double champ does what the fuck he wants, indeed. It's only a lesson if you learn from it, and there's a lot you can learn about McGregor from that quote right there.

Why Your Ankle Sprain Won't Heal: PRP and Medical Next Steps

Ankle sprains are common, but when they fail to heal properly, they can turn into a chronic problem. Understanding why your ankle sprain isn't getting better and exploring advanced treatments like PRP for ankle sprain can help you get back on your feet more quickly.

Why Some Ankle Sprains Don't HealEven a seemingly mild sprain can linger if underlying issues aren't addressed. Common reasons include:

• Incomplete initial healing – Returning to activity too soon can prevent proper ligament repair – Inadequate rest, ice, compression, elevation (RICE) phase

• Persistent joint instability – Damaged ligaments don't regain full strength or elasticity – Micro-tears and scar tissue limit ligament function

• Poor blood supply – Ligaments receive less blood flow than muscles, slowing repair – Smoking, diabetes or vascular disease can compound the problem

• Misdiagnosis or missed injury – High-ankle (syndesmotic) sprains, cartilage damage or bone bruises may be overlooked – Peroneal tendon injuries often occur alongside lateral ankle sprains

• Repeated re-injury – Inadequate rehabilitation and balance training raise the risk of re-sprain – Uneven surfaces or improper footwear contribute

• Underlying structural issues – Flat feet, high arches or limb length differences place extra stress on your ankle – Joint hypermobility disorders can delay recovery

Key Signs That Something Else Is Going OnIf your ankle still hurts after 6–8 weeks of standard care, consider whether you have:

A high-ankle (syndesmotic) sprain

Osteochondral lesions (cartilage or bone fragments inside the joint)

Chronic ankle instability (feeling like your ankle "gives way")

Tendon tears or peroneal tendon subluxation

Stress fractures in the nearby bones

If any of these sound familiar, you may need further evaluation.

Medical Next Steps: A Stepwise Approach

Re-evaluate the Diagnosis• Speak to an orthopedic specialist or sports medicine physician• Consider advanced imaging: – MRI for ligaments, tendons, cartilage lesions – CT scan for subtle fractures or bone fragments – Diagnostic ultrasound for real-time tendon assessment

Optimize Conservative Care• Physical therapy focused on: – Proprioception and balance drills – Strengthening of peroneal, tibialis posterior and calf muscles – Functional exercises mimicking your sport or daily activities• Bracing or taping to support healing ligaments• Activity modification: low-impact cardio (swimming, cycling) until stability improves

Consider Regenerative Treatments• PRP for ankle sprain to boost ligament and tendon repair• Platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) as an alternative with slower release of growth factors• Hyaluronic acid injections for joint lubrication in cases with cartilage wear

Evaluate for Surgical Options• Ligament repair or reconstruction may be needed with complete ligament tears or chronic instability• Arthroscopic debridement if cartilage fragments or osteochondral lesions are causing pain• Tendon repair for peroneal or posterior tibial tendon tears

Long-Term Maintenance• Continue balance and strength training even after symptoms resolve• Wear supportive footwear or ankle braces during high-risk activities• Address biomechanical issues: orthotics for flat feet or high arches

Voltaren Gel: 5 Important Things Doctors Wish You Knew

Voltaren gel (diclofenac sodium topical gel) is a popular over-the-counter and prescription treatment for joint and muscle pain. Below are five key insights—backed by clinical evidence and expert opinion—that doctors wish every patient understood before using Voltaren gel.

1. How Voltaren Gel Works—and When It Helps

Voltaren gel is a topical nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). Instead of swallowing a pill, you apply it directly to your skin over the painful area. Clinical studies, including a Cochrane review of topical NSAIDs for chronic musculoskeletal pain (Derry et al., 2016), show that Voltaren gel can:

Reduce pain and improve function in osteoarthritis of the knee and hand

Work as effectively as some oral NSAIDs, but with lower overall drug levels in the bloodstream

Provide relief within a week, with maximal benefit often seen in 2–3 weeks of consistent use

Common uses include:

Knee, hand or elbow osteoarthritis

Strains, sprains and minor sports injuries

Tendinitis or bursitis

Doctors often recommend Voltaren gel for mild-to-moderate pain when you prefer to avoid or minimize oral NSAID side effects.

2. Applying Voltaren Gel the Right Way

Proper application maximizes benefit and minimizes side effects.

Clean and dry the skin – wash the area with mild soap, pat dry.

Measure your dose – use the provided dosing card or follow package instructions (e.g., 2 g of gel per knee, up to 4 times daily).

Apply a thin layer – gently rub it in until the gel disappears.

Wash your hands – unless you're treating your hands, to avoid accidental contact with eyes or mouth.

Avoid covering – don't bandage, wrap or apply heat over the treated area unless directed by your doctor.

Liver disease: People with cirrhosis or significant hepatic impairment may have altered diclofenac metabolism (D'Amico et al., 2006). Even topical use can raise liver enzymes.

Kidney disease: Reduced renal function increases the risk of NSAID-induced kidney injury.

Heart disease or high blood pressure: NSAIDs can worsen hypertension and increase cardiovascular risk.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: Avoid in the third trimester; discuss risks with your doctor if you're pregnant or nursing.

Children and adolescents: Voltaren gel is not approved for use in those under 14 years of age.

5. When to Seek Medical Advice and Next Steps

Voltaren gel can be a helpful part of your pain-management plan, but it's not a cure. If your pain:

Persists or worsens after 2–3 weeks of proper use

Is accompanied by swelling, redness, or deformity

Follows a severe injury or fall

Comes with fever, chills, or unexplained weight loss

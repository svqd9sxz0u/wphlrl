# How to Do the Incline Reverse Bench Crunch

The hanging leg raise may be the holy grail of core training. There’s just one giant problem with the move: It’s ridiculously tough, and most guys can’t do more than one or two reps with perfect form.

That’s why Men’s Health Fitness Director BJ Gaddour recommends trying the incline reverse bench crunch.

The exercise mimics the same pattern as the hanging leg raise—pummeling your lower abs and activating your upper back—but removes a level of difficultly since you are lying on a bench.

Do as many reps as possible for 3 to 5 sets, resting 1 minute between each set. If you feel the movement in your lower back instead of your abs, stop.

You could also use the exercise as an active recovery movement between heavy lifts like squats and deadlifts, or do it as an abs finisher at the end of your regular workout.

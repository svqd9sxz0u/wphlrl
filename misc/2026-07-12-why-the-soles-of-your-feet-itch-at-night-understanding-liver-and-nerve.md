Why Itching Feels Worse at Night

Itch intensity often spikes after dark due to several natural and environmental factors:

Circadian rhythms: Our skin's sensitivity to itch-triggering chemicals (like histamine) rises in the evening.

Lower cortisol: Cortisol, a natural anti-inflammatory hormone, dips overnight, making it easier to feel irritated.

Reduced distractions: Quiet surroundings and fewer activities draw attention inward, making you more aware of mild itches.

Warm bedroom: Heat stimulates skin nerve endings, worsening the sensation.

Liver Links to Foot Itching

When the liver isn't processing waste products effectively, pruritus (itching) can result. In some liver conditions, bile acids build up in the bloodstream and settle in the skin, especially on the palms and soles.

Key liver-related causes:

Intrahepatic (within the liver) or extrahepatic (bile duct blockage)

Common in conditions like primary biliary cholangitis (PBC)

Scar tissue narrows bile ducts, leading to cholestasis

Certain medications hinder bile excretion

Chronic liver inflammation or advanced scarring

Typical symptoms accompanying liver-linked itching:

Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)

Dark urine and pale stools

Fatigue, abdominal pain or swelling

Unexplained weight loss

What you can do:

Get liver function tests (LFTs) and serum bile acid levels

Discuss medication review with your doctor (some drugs can exacerbate itching)

Follow up on imaging (ultrasound, MRCP) if bile duct obstruction is suspected

Nerve-Related Causes of Foot Itch

Nerves in the feet and lower legs carry both pain and itch signals. When these nerves become injured or compressed, the brain may misinterpret signals as itching rather than pain.

Common neuropathic contributors:

Diabetes-related nerve damage

Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency

Other Potential Contributors

While liver and nerve issues can be serious, don't overlook more common, treatable factors:

Dry skin (xerosis): Especially in cool, low-humidity months

Eczema or psoriasis: Often surfaces on soles as red, flaky patches

Fungal infections: Athlete's foot can cause nightly itching

Contact dermatitis: Irritation from shoes, socks or laundry detergents

Circulation problems: Venous stasis may lead to itching in lower legs and feet

When to Seek Professional Help

Most causes of itchy soles can be managed at home, but certain red flags warrant prompt medical attention:

New yellowing of skin or eyes

Dark urine or pale stools

Unexplained bruising or bleeding

Severe, persistent itching that disrupts sleep and daily life

Accompanying neurological symptoms: muscle weakness, numbness or pain

Signs of infection: redness, swelling, warmth or fever

Take-Home Points

Nighttime itch on the soles can stem from internal (liver, nerve) or external (dry skin, fungal) factors.

Liver-related itching often pairs with jaundice, dark urine and fatigue.

Neuropathic itch features burning, tingling or altered sensation.

Simple home measures—moisturizing, cooler room, breathable socks—can ease mild cases.

Don't hesitate to seek medical evaluation for persistent or severe itching.

Fitness trainer and bodybuilding coach Eugene Teo is well aware that for many people, the goal of lifting is to grow some serious muscles. That's why he has been sharing a series about the "2 Best Exercises" he loves that target specific areas of your body for muscle growth. Teo has shared the two best exercises for building bigger biceps, his favorite ab growing exercises, exercises to grow your quads, to grow your hamstrings, to train and target your triceps, and more. But of course, Teo knows that lifting is not just about looks and growth.

[Exercises to Build](https://satietybrief.china-akan.com/)

"Training a muscle is not just about increasing its size or improving its looks," he says. "Training a muscle is about taking a muscle, all of your muscles, through as much of a contractile range as possible under some kind of meaningful stimulus."

By neglecting this principle, Teo says you can inhibit your joints, movement, and training in the long run. With that said, he jumps into the topic of the day: his top exercises to grow the muscles in your chest.

Low Incline Dumbbell Press

"I prefer these over any type of pressing," says Teo, before acknowledging that most chest training features some type of pressing movement. The difference here is in the dumbbells.

"I believe dumbbells are better over barbells because of the freedom that it creates at the grip wrist and elbow. It allows you to naturally customize the grips to your personal structure."

Teo says that the human body is not designed symmetrically, so a fixed straight bar doesn't accommodate for optimal movement patterns. Adjusting to work under a bar can lead to unnecessary "wear and tear" on the body, especially on the elbows and shoulders.

He also believes the incline bench is also optimal position for your shoulder joint.

"It allows you tot take your arm into a degree of external rotation which tends to provide a lot more comfort, stability and usually strength as well while also allowing your chest fibers to remain active through the entire movement," says Teo.

Teo prefers an angle between 15 to 30 degrees, but it comes down to your personal sternal angle, rib cage size, and the arch in your back.

Exercise 2: The Pushup

Teo goes for a classic for his second choice. "I have found them to be extremely important, beneficial and extremely underrated from a chest development and movement capacity perspective," says Teo.

To create more resistance, Teo likes to use resistance bands. He loops them into a figure-8 and puts it across his back, slotting his elbows through the loops.

"This allows you to stimulate your chest in that shortened position," says Teo.

He also loves pushups, particularly with bands, because they force you to have a degree of protraction or rounding in the upper back when locking out. This creates freedom in your shoulder blades to move in natural pathways, which is different from bench-based exercise, where shoulders are often locked in place for the shortest possible range of motion.

Teo also likes how pushups train the serratus anterior, which he says is "so important for shoulder and shoulder blade mechanics."

A final note on pushups: he suggests doing pushups after the dumbbell incline press. That's because you'll be more likely to tire out your core than your chest during the movement if you lead with pushups—so try to program your presses first.

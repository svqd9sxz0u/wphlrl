An apparent and continued act of revenge by an ex-boyfriend is at the center of a lawsuit against Grindr, the dating app for gay and bisexual men. Matthew Herrick says that 1,100 men have showed up at his home and the restaurant where he works expecting to have sex with him. He says his ex-boyfriend, who he also met on Grindr, has been making fake profiles using his images since October 2016. (Here are 3 other terrible things that men do on dating apps.) And because it hasn’t stopped, Herrick is suing Grindr, CNN reports.

The lawsuit was originally filed in New York but has been moved to a federal court, per Grindr’s request. It raises questions about tech company’s responsibilities in cases of impersonation and harassment. “What are Grindr’s legal responsibilities,” Aaron Mackey, a legal fellow at the Electronic Frontier Foundation, posited to CNN. “And what are its corporate and ethical responsibilities to its users when it learns that its platform is being abused in this way?”

Herrick says as many of 16 people a day will show up looking to have sex with him. (This is how you can find a date on an app the right way, though.) He’s made more than 100 reports to flag fake profiles but has only received the generic “Thank you for your report” replies from Grindr. However, another app named Scruff has removed the profiles and banned the IP addresses behind them, according to the suit.

Despite the clear ugliness (and pettiness) of this case, Grindr may prevail because of the Communications Decency Act, which gives broad protections to companies from being held responsible for its user-generated content. Grindr was found not liable in a previous case in which a man was arrested for having sex with a minor he met on the app.

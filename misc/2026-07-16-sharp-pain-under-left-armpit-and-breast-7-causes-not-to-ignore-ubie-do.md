Sharp pain under the left armpit that radiates to the side of the breast is often linked to benign soft tissue conditions. Two common causes include nodular fasciitis, a rapidly growing but non-cancerous lump in the connective tissue, and axillary web syndrome, which produces tight, painful cord-like structures under the arm—frequently after lymph node surgery or injury.

While these causes are typically not serious, sharp underarm and breast pain can also point to other conditions ranging from muscle strain to infections or, less commonly, more serious issues. Because symptoms overlap across many conditions, self-diagnosis can be unreliable and delay proper care.

There are a few potential reasons for experiencing sharp pain in these areas. One possibility is nodular fasciitis, a benign condition where a small lump of tissue develops in the axillary tail of the breast. Although it is not cancerous, this growth can cause a sharp, localized pain. Another explanation might be axillary web syndrome. This condition involves the formation of tight, cord-like bands under the skin which can sometimes occur even without surgery or other interventions. These bands may shorten or tighten, pulling on the tissue and causing a sharp, sometimes sudden pain that radiates from the armpit to the side of the breast. In some cases, similar pain may also be seen after procedures involving the armpit, where needle placement or manipulation of the lymph nodes contributes to discomfort. If the pain is persistent or if you notice any new lumps, swelling, or other changes, it's important to see a doctor.

Doctor's Note: Should Black Women Get a DEXA Scan Before Starting GLP-1 Injections?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Black women starting GLP-1 injections should consider a baseline DEXA scan to measure bone density before major weight loss begins. This is especially important for those with risk factors such as early menopause, a family history of osteoporosis, or long-term steroid use. Rapid weight loss can accelerate bone density decline, making early assessment critical for prevention.

Key steps include:

Fracture risk assessment using tools like FRAX

Bone-healthy nutrition, prioritizing calcium, vitamin D, and adequate protein

Weight-bearing and resistance exercise to preserve bone strength

Follow-up DEXA monitoring to track changes during treatment

Because bone health risks vary widely from person to person, understanding your individual profile is the smartest first step before starting or continuing GLP-1 therapy. A quick, private evaluation of your symptoms and risk factors can help you decide whether to pursue a DEXA scan, adjust your nutrition, or speak with a specialist. Explanation

GLP-1 agonists (often called "GLP-1s," e.g. semaglutide, liraglutide) have become popular for type 2 diabetes and weight management. As more patients consider these injections, questions arise about potential effects on bone health—especially in Black women, who traditionally have higher bone density but still face unique risks. Below, we review what the research tells us about GLP-1 and bone density scans, and help you decide whether a DEXA scan makes sense before you start therapy.

Why Bone Health and GLP-1 Matter

GLP-1 agonists work by:

Slowing gastric emptying

Increasing insulin secretion

Suppressing appetite

Weight loss is common—sometimes 5–15% of body weight in a few months. However, rapid or significant weight loss can lead to bone-mineral loss. In the general population, studies have shown:

Small decreases in bone density with some GLP-1s, though data are mixed

No clear, large increase in fracture risk in short-term trials

Weight-loss–associated bone loss may be more related to calorie restriction than GLP-1 itself

Key point: GLP-1s may indirectly affect bones through weight loss, appetite changes, or hormonal shifts.

Black Women and Bone Density: What's Different?

Black women on average have:

Higher peak bone mass

Lower fracture rates compared with White women

That said, they still face osteoporosis and fracture risk, particularly after menopause or with other risk factors. Reasons to consider bone density evaluation include:

Early menopause

Family history of osteoporosis or hip fracture

Chronic corticosteroid use

Rheumatoid arthritis

Low body mass index (BMI < 20 kg/m²)

Smoking, excessive alcohol use

Personal history of fragility fracture

While Black women start with a "bone density advantage," age and weight-loss therapies can change that picture.

When to Consider a DEXA Scan

A DEXA scan (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) measures bone mineral density at the hip and spine. It gives you a T-score (compared to young adults) and a Z-score (age-matched). Guidelines (e.g., by the National Osteoporosis Foundation) recommend screening:

All women aged 65 and older

Postmenopausal women under 65 with risk factors (see above)

Anyone with a fragility fracture

If you're a Black woman planning GLP-1 therapy, think about a DEXA scan if you have:

Planned weight loss over 10% of your baseline body weight

Two or more of the risk factors listed above

A family history of hip fractures or osteoporosis in a first-degree relative

Symptoms like new bone pain or back pain without injury

Even if you don't meet formal criteria, a baseline DEXA can be a helpful reference. It lets your provider track changes over time if you lose a lot of weight on GLP-1 therapy.

A 2023 review in the Journal of Bone and Mineral Research found no consistent increase in fracture risk with GLP-1s, but recommended more long-term studies.

Weight loss itself—regardless of method—often leads to 1–3% bone-density loss per 10% of weight lost.

Guidelines from the American Society for Bone and Mineral Research suggest bone-density screening when weight loss exceeds 10% of body weight, especially in postmenopausal women.

Putting It All Together

Assess Your Risk

Review personal/ family fracture history and risk factors.

Estimate expected weight loss on GLP-1 therapy.

Discuss With Your Doctor

Ask if a baseline DEXA scan makes sense for your risk profile.

Plan follow-up scans if you lose significant weight.

Optimize Bone Health

Ensure adequate calcium, vitamin D, protein.

Engage in regular weight-bearing and resistance exercises.

Address modifiable risks (smoking, alcohol, fall hazards).

Monitor Over Time

Repeat DEXA as recommended (every 1–2 years if high risk).

Report any new bone pain, fractures or height loss promptly.

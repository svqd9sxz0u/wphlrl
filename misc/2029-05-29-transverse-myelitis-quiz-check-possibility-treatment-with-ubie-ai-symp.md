What is Transverse Myelitis?

Transverse myelitis is an inflammation of both sides of one section of the spinal cord. There is often damage to the covering of nerve cell fibers (myelin), which interrupts the messages from the spinal cord nerves to the rest of the body. Causes include infections and immune system disorders that attack the body's tissues.

Typical Symptoms of Transverse Myelitis

Fever that started after cold or stomach symptoms

History of connective tissue, autoimmune or rheumatic disease

Tingling or pricking numbness in the legs even when keeping still

Numbness or tingling in my toes

Both legs feel weak or difficult to move

Reduced sensitivity to feeling pain

Numbness anywhere from the knees to the ankles

Weakness in your arms or legs

Diagnostic Questions for Transverse Myelitis

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Did your fever start after having cold or stomach symptoms?

Have you ever been diagnosed with a connective tissue, autoimmune, or rheumatic disease?

Do you have tingling or numbness in your legs even when you're not moving?

Do you experience numbness or tingling in your toes?

Are both of your legs feeling weak or difficult to move?

Treatment of Transverse Myelitis

There is no effective cure for transverse myelitis, but many people recover from it. Treatments include medication to suppress inflammation, relieve pain, and rehabilitation. Those with severe symptoms may need to be hospitalized.

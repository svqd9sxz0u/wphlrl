# I Confessed I Was A Control Freak And It Changed Everything. You Might Be Surprised How Freedom Follows Honesty.

WHEN YOU ARRIVE at the 2,600-acre Rising Circle Ranch outside of Santa Fe, New Mexico, your sense of well-being already begins to kick in. It’s not just the vast open blue skies of New Mexico and the smell of the juniper pinons, but the tranquil and welcoming setting of the adobe-inspired environment that awaits you.

It's the home of M/E/A (Modern Elder Academy), what founder and executive chairman Chip Conley calls "the world’s first midlife wisdom school." Founded in 2018 in Baja, California to help individuals explore midlife transitions, the Santa Fe location opened in May 2024 to expand the mission to help those who are looking to reimagine what the second half of their lives may look like.

It's part of a burgeoning industry of non-academic and academically oriented programs, as well as support groups that are tapping into what many call a new midlife awakening.

With over 30 percent of the U.S. population over 50 years old and the first Millennials turning 50 in four years, there will be over 130 million people all thinking about what they may want in their future, as they anticipate living much longer lives than previous generations.

If you are in midlife and committed to a lifestyle that includes a preventive health approach, a fitness regimen, a focus on nutrition, good sleep habits and a sense of community, you may live to 90 or even 100 years old. What will you do with all those extra years? You might start a new career, launch a business, run your first marathon, tap into a new purpose or start a new relationship.

The gift of the New Longevity era is that the possibilities are endless. Conley and his team, along with the other efforts have tapped into the new longevity zeitgeist.

“We are not a university or college. We are a different experience with many options that include three- and four-day on- site workshops and lots of online midlife transition programs,” explained Conley when I sat down with him in the cozy library at Juniper Hall, one of the main buildings on the ranch’s campus.

Although I had met him during visits to Santa Fe, where I’m a part-time resident, I wasn’t sure if attending one of M/E/A’s one hundred annual public workshops that include “Navigating Transitions” and “Cultivating Wisdom” was right for me.

But when he invited me to become a guest-faculty to lead a four-day workshop called ROAR Into Midlife: Build Your Bold Second Act with him as co-leader, it was an intriguing idea. The curriculum would be developed from my book ROAR Into the Second Half of Your Life, in addition to some of the proven topics from the M/E/A playbook.

I’d also be joining the ranks of such male notables as Arthur Brooks, Dan Buettner and the poet and philosopher David Whyte, who have all been teachers at M/E/A’s campus, as have many well-known female authors like Elizabeth Gilbert and Martha Beck.

When I agreed to do it, I didn’t anticipate that it would have such a personal impact for me, as well as the 17 individuals who signed up for the session.

Conley’s idea for M/E/A came after he finished a stint as the “modern elder” at Airbnb. That gig had followed his longtime career as an entrepreneur/owner of the Joie de Vivre boutique hotel chain that he sold in 2010 at the age of 50.

It ignited a passion in him to think about midlife in a new way, spawning several books, including the Midlife Manifesto and Learning to Love Midlife.

[Changed Everything You](https://allpepay.com/blog/1312711755.html)

Since 2018, over 7,500 people have attended M/E/A workshops. Many of them, including my group, continue to stay in touch with their cohorts, join M/E/A regional chapters and affinity groups that might include Caregiving, Career Change and more.

“The average age of the men who join us is 52, while women are around 56. What we’ve learned is that men start to feel a little bit irrelevant around 50. Work changes and the lack of a social infrastructure to deal with it often times motivates them,” said Conley, adding, “It is often some type of event like a divorce or losing a job or career that brings them to us.”

Universities have also tapped into creating programs that help people at midlife. Two of the best known are the Stanford Distinguished Careers Institute and the Harvard Advanced Leadership Initiative, both full time one year on campus programs that are usually for post-career individuals.

Others have emerged in recent years, like the University of Chicago’s Leadership and Society Initiative (LSI). Their offerings include the LSI Design Pathway, also a full academic year on campus, including a one-week travel study on one of UChicago’s global campuses. Diana Petty, Executive Director of LSI, announced a new program in spring 2025 called Imagine Pathway for those who are still working but want to start on their own midlife transition.

“In January 2026, we’ll have an inaugural cohort of 35-40 Imagine Fellows," explained Petty. “It’s a curated curriculum on purpose, contribution and personal wellness.” For those still in busy careers, the program includes four-day retreats four times a year on campus, with online learning between retreats. According to Petty, the Imagine Pathway received more than 300 candidacies for the launch program.

At the University of Minnesota, the Midlife Academy launched “Cultivating Purpose,” their first program with 25 individuals in the fall of 2025. Kate Schaefers, Ph.D., of the Midlife Academy said, “We have a new course, Next Chapter Reset: Creating Momentum for the Spring of 2026. We are trying to build a community for those in midlife, adding Zoom versions and a podcast to our curriculum.”

Programs like Change Makers at CU Denver and the Experienced Leaders Initiative (ELI) at Yale are also examples of other programs that don’t require full-time on campus commitments. To learn more about what other academic midlife transition programs might be available, check out The Nexel Collaborative.

While there are other non-academic retreat centers like the Esalen Institute and Omega that let you explore self-discovery, according to M/E/A’s Conley, his group is the only effort that focuses exclusively on midlife transitions with practical coursework to help the process along.

For those who want something a bit more unstructured, there are also men’s support groups like MENSGROUP, founded by Sean Galla. According to him, most of the men who attend the hundreds of online community sessions are thirty-five to sixty-year-olds. The Midlife Male, ManKind Project and Elevation Barn are other groups that offer personal growth and development, some for men of all ages.

THE NIGHT BEFORE we started our M/E/A four-day workshop, our group had a meet-and-greet to get to know each other. A mix of men and women, the guys included a former president of a data company, an intervention counselor, a former restaurant owner, a Canadian financial executive and others. There were also two married couples who decided to do the program together. At the opener, we broke the ice by announcing our name and saying, “If you really knew me…”

When it was my turn, I surprised myself by becoming very emotional at the recent loss of my father, who was my biggest champion.

My response had been, “If you really knew me you would know that I’m grieving the loss of my dad,” but when I choked up, I realized that I hadn’t really let myself grieve over his death.

Throughout our four days together, we explored the importance of purpose, finding joy, going back to our younger selves to rediscover what excited us that we might reclaim moving forward. There were a number of self-revealing exercises that opened us up to each other in ways that we might not do so with our family and friends.

We were telling strangers things about ourselves that often times we don’t even admit to ourselves. In one of the more intense interactions, I acknowledged my own obsession with having to control everything and everyone around me. “I am a control-freak,” I confessed to the group, expounding on why that wasn’t such a good trait.

As we shared our successes, failures and dreams with each other, it was both liberating and insightful, especially to watch other men let their guards down to help them find a path for their next chapter.

Shayne Anderson, one of our group members and CEO of SHIFT Recovery Services, a case management company said, “I got married at 52, graduated college at 53, had my first child at 54 and my second at 56. I’m living proof that middle age has no barriers," he said. "At the workshop, I rediscovered that growth doesn’t stop at any age; it simply evolves. I left with renewed clarity, a lighter heart, and a deeper appreciation for presence in my life, my relationships and my calling.”

We started each day with a meditation/movement session led by the inspirational Lee Johnson or went out on a group hike. Every day, that was followed by the “Wisdom Circle” that allowed each of us to express what we might be thinking about in our lives like the desire to leave a corporate job or to find deeper friendships.

The structure of the day included two separate two-hour sessions that focused on particular topics like ROAR’s life layering concept that is an action plan for building out new self-identifiers beyond partner, parent, and profession. As our group bonded, we shared meals, talks around a campfire, went for runs, and rode horses on the ranch.

By Friday, many members of the group already declared what they might take as a first step to their next chapter. Some made a commitment to developing an off-ramp to their corporate job, while others focused on wanting to find love again.

[Everything You Might Be](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/blog/7637832576.html)

Farid Salloum, a longtime restaurateur in Baltimore, who had already closed his highly popular Baba’s Kitchen, came to the session to explore what to do at this significant crossroads in his life. “The program opened up my mind on what’s possible for my transition and afforded me the tools to act on it. The connections made in the cohort have been on a special level that are unparalleled with my existing friendships,” he said.

Although Conley and I led the workshop, facilitated by the talented Darby Hillman, I too left the four-day session with a renewed sense of myself, including a pledge to be less controlling and to continue to help others who were at a midlife transition.

Whether you decide to pursue a more academic route, a men’s support group or the M/E/A approach, there’s one thing that rang true for me during the experience.

Spending time at a midlife program is an incredible gift to give to yourself to reflect and explore what’s important (or not) to you as you move into midlife. To open yourself up and share a sense of reinvention, vulnerability and support with a group of like-minded men and women may be just what you need to get you on your own course for a new vision of the second half of your life.

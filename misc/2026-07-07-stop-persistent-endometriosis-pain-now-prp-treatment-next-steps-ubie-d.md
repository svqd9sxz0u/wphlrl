Endometriosis is a chronic condition in which tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus. Many people find their pain never fully disappears, even after multiple treatments. Understanding why endometriosis pain won't stop—and exploring new options like PRP for endometriosis—can help you take informed next steps in managing your health.

Why Endometriosis Pain PersistsEndometriosis pain can be relentless for several reasons:

• Lesion activity – Endometriosis lesions bleed and inflame with each menstrual cycle, irritating nearby nerves and tissues. – New lesions can form over time, maintaining a cycle of inflammation.

• Chronic inflammation – The body mounts an immune response to misplaced tissue, producing chemical signals that heighten pain. – Inflammation can spread, affecting organs beyond the pelvis.

• Nerve sensitization – Repeated irritation trains nerves to fire more easily, so even mild triggers can feel severe. – This "wind-up" effect means pain signals continue after the initial cause subsides.

• Adhesions and scarring – Lesions often lead to bands of scar tissue (adhesions) that tether organs together. – Adhesions can cause constant pulling and pressure, contributing to persistent discomfort.

• Hormonal fluctuations – Estrogen fuels lesion growth, so natural hormone shifts during your cycle can worsen pain. – Hormonal treatments may reduce flare-ups, but stopping therapy often allows pain to recur.

Conventional Treatment OptionsMost care plans combine symptom relief with efforts to slow disease progression:

• Pain medications – Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs) ease inflammation but don't stop lesion growth. – Prescription painkillers can help short-term but carry risks of tolerance and side effects.

• Hormonal therapy – Birth control pills, patches or rings can thin lesions by suppressing ovulation. – Progestin-only treatments and GnRH agonists create a low-estrogen state, but may cause menopause-like symptoms.

• Surgery – Laparoscopic removal or ablation of lesions provides relief for many, yet recurrence rates can be up to 50% within five years. – Repeated surgeries increase the risk of adhesions and organ damage.

Emerging Therapy: PRP for EndometriosisPlatelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy is gaining attention as a novel approach to address inflammation and promote healing.

What is PRP?PRP is derived from your own blood. A small sample is spun in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets—cells rich in growth factors. These growth factors:

• Stimulate tissue repair• Reduce inflammation• Encourage new blood vessel growth (angiogenesis)

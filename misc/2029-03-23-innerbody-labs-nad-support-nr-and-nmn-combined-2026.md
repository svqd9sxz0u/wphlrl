Potential benefits of raising NAD+ levels

NAD+ is a coenzyme that helps fuel and manage metabolic activity in the mitochondria — the portion of a cell that turns the food you eat into energy. That energy powers everything from muscle contractions to neural signaling, and healthy mitochondria tend to keep healthy cells. As you age, NAD+ levels decline, creating an imbalance between NAD+ and NADH.14 An overabundance of NADH is seen both in aging patients and in people after a night of heavy drinking, so it's related to both acute inflammation and illness and chronic, long-term physical decline.15 16

It stands to reason that restoring balance between NAD+ and NADH, or even having a greater abundance of NAD+, could reap anti-aging benefits. To date, the benefits seen in studies looking at increased NAD+ levels include:17

A crash course in NAD+ production

Understanding how the ingredients in NAD+ Support come together to increase NAD+ levels sustainably requires some understanding of NAD+ metabolism.

NAD+ precursors almost all rely on one metabolic system for NAD+ production: the salvage pathway. The other two pathways for producing NAD+ are the Preiss-Handler and de novo synthesis pathways, which rely on dietary vitamin B3 and tryptophan, respectively, and only account for about 15% of all NAD+ production.2 That means that 85% of all NAD+ production occurs via the salvage pathway.7

Here’s what the salvage pathway looks like in action, with inputs illustrated for supplemental NR, NMN, TMG, and spermidine:

Image by Innerbody

In the salvage pathway, NAM combines with 5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate before becoming NMN, then turning into NAD+. When you take NMN, it jumps ahead of NAM and turns into NAD+ directly. When you take NR, it turns into NMN before becoming NAD+.2

You’ll notice TMG and spermidine in the diagram above, as well. TMG’s role is in fueling NAM’s conversion to NMN. Without enough methyl donors like TMG in the picture, increases in NAD+ production can fade after around 12 weeks.8

Meanwhile, spermidine fends off NNMT, which is trying to divert a bit of NAM from the salvage pathway and repurpose it for what ultimately might do more harm than good.9 11

With these four ingredients in supplement form (NMN, NR, TMG, and spermidine), NAD+ production can increase and sustain itself.

Why combine NMN and NR?

As we alluded to above, NMN and NR enter the salvage pathway at different points. They also rely on different processes and transporters to get into cells before becoming NAD+.3 By supplementing with both, NAD+ production efficiency should be greater than if you were to supplement with just one or the other.

The only problem with this hypothesis is that it hasn’t been tested directly. There aren’t any studies pitting combined precursor administration against a monotherapy of one or the other.

Despite the data that’s missing from the research landscape, there are compelling reasons to combine precursors, and they come from the studies of each individual ingredient: NMN research has revealed certain secondary effects that NR studies haven’t produced, and vice versa.

For example, NMN’s abilities to improve sleep and telomere length haven’t been reproduced in NR studies.19 20 And NR’s ability to improve biomarkers of neurodegenerative disease hasn’t been reproduced in NMN research.21 It’s entirely possible that these effects are linked more to increased NAD+ levels than to either precursor, but if you look at where the science is today, taking both should offer a greater range of potential benefits than taking just one.

NAD+ Support FAQ

Can NAD+ Support stop aging?

Nothing will stop the aging process, but if you look at aging as an accumulation of cellular damage over time, it becomes clear that improving cellular health might slow down the rate at which you age physically.1 The roles NAD+ plays in your cellular health make it likely that increasing NAD+ levels can decrease your pace of aging and improve your healthspan.2

How long does it take for NAD+ Support to work?

NAD+ Support will raise your NAD+ levels after a single dose, as both NMN and NR research has shown that one dose can increase NAD.3 However, it will likely be several weeks, if not months, before you feel the effects of that sustained increase with daily consumption. And if you’re generally healthy, you may only notice that others around you seem to be aging faster than you after many years of use.

Can raising NAD+ levels cause cancer?

Scientists don’t know yet whether increased NAD+ levels correlate directly with an increased cancer risk. NAD+ has both hindered and exacerbated tumor growth in separate studies, so a lot more research is needed to understand its role in tumorigenesis and metastasis.30 31

Can’t I just take some vitamin B3 instead?

Vitamin B3 does contribute to increased NAD+ levels (NR is technically a form of B3), but the doses required are high, and the metabolic pathway that relies on it to produce NAD+ only accounts for a fraction of total NAD+ production. By comparison, the pathway that utilizes both NR and NMN to produce NAD+ accounts for 85% of NAD+ production, making it a more viable target for treatment.7

Which is better, NMN or NR?

Both NMN and NR will raise NAD+ levels. There’s some evidence that one precursor might be better for a specific secondary goal than the other, like NMN’s potential to increase telomere lengths or NR’s potential to support brain health.19 21 To get the full suite of benefits associated with either, it may be best to take both.

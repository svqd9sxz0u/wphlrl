# The Total Guide to Keeping Your Eyes Happy

We’ve learned how to self-optimize almost every part of our bodies, from muscles to mind. But it’s easy to overlook one of the most used (and useful) body parts of all — our eyes.

That's a missed opportunity to keep our vision on track. After all, we have a ton of habits that aren’t ideal for vision — staring at small screens all day, blasting the heat or AC, forgoing UV-protecting glasses. Taking care of your eyes now helps prevent problems in the future, says Rupe Hansra, O.D., an optometrist with Visionworks.

And everyday habits make a serious difference. “Prevention is the best medicine,” he says. “Be proactive with a combination of exercise and diet and protection from harmful light. What’s good for your heart is good for your eyes — diet and exercise.” Here, some essential strategies for keeping your eyes as healthy as they can be (hint: prime on the list is a great pair of glasses from Cole Haan).

Go easy on the blue light

It’s no secret we’re all looking at screens plenty these days — at work, at home, even at night as we binge our favorite shows (and, admit it, scroll Instagram while half paying attention to the latest episode). All that device time can mess with our vision. “Excessive screen time can cause headaches, fatigue, and general discomfort,” Dr. Hansra says, noting the average American spends seven hours per day (!) looking at a screen.

He suggests purchasing eyewear designed specifically for your computer, which can help mitigate the harmful effects of screen time. What kind of pair is up to the job? “Oftentimes they have a slight prescription set at the distance of your computer, an anti-glare coating which cuts down 90% of reflections, and a blue light blocker for enhanced comfort and clarity,” he says. And don’t forget that brands like Cole Haan have many options that combine style and function. “As a rule of thumb, for those over 40, they might feel the screen is clearer right away," explains Dr. Hansra, "while those under 40 will notice a significant decrease in symptoms.”

Get ahead of aging

The biggest obstacle our eyes face is, well, the passing of time — which happens to everyone. The best way to deal with aging is smart defense. As we age, our eyes and sight naturally degrade. According to Dr. Hansra, the primary sign you'll notice is things might not look as clear when you're driving at night. If this is the case, it’s time to visit your eye doctor for a thorough check. The solution to this annoying (and possibly dangerous) problem may be as simple as a new pair of glasses.

Look out for your sight

Getting your eyes examined annually is essential — and not just for your vision.

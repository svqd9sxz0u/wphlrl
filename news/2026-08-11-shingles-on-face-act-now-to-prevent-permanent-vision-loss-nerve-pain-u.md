Shingles on the face can lead to serious long-term complications, including persistent nerve pain (postherpetic neuralgia) and vision problems that may result in permanent damage if the eye is involved.

Key facts about facial shingles:

Nerve pain can last months or years after the rash heals

Eye involvement (herpes zoster ophthalmicus) can cause corneal damage, glaucoma, or vision loss

Early antiviral treatment within 72 hours reduces complication risk

Prompt medical evaluation is critical when shingles appears near the eyes or forehead

Explanation

Shingles on the face, also known as Herpes Zoster Ophthalmicus, can lead to serious complications. One of the main issues is long-term nerve pain, which is called postherpetic neuralgia. This is when the pain from shingles lasts for a long time, even after the rash is gone.

Another problem is with the eyes. Shingles can affect the eyes and cause vision problems. This happens because the virus can attack the nerves around the eyes. If not treated, it can lead to serious eye issues, including vision loss.

It is important to see a doctor if you have shingles on your face to get the right treatment.
